# Game Vision — VANO: Last Ride

## 1. Elevator pitch

VANO: Last Ride is a 3D endless downhill adventure for mobile (Android first) where the player rides a snowboard freely across an open snowy mountain, steering, drifting, jumping, and sliding, transforming between snow vehicles on the fly, collecting crystals and avoiding obstacles as speed and difficulty rise. It is built around three pillars: **fun**, **retention**, and **revenue** — in that order of priority, but never sacrificing one for another.

## 2. Vision statement

Deliver a mobile downhill experience that feels fast, free, and skill-based, with an original cartoon-3D identity distinct from generic AI-generated games and from direct copies of existing downhill titles. The game should be immediately playable in short sessions yet deep enough to support daily challenges, friends challenges, and leaderboards.

## 3. Core pillars

1. **Fun first.** Movement must feel satisfying: smooth steering, weighty drifting, responsive jumping and sliding, increasing speed. If movement is not fun, nothing else matters.
2. **Retention through variety and mastery.** Procedural endless terrain, daily and friends challenges on identical tracks, weekly and all-time leaderboards, vehicle transformations with balanced durations, cosmetic progression.
3. **Sustainable revenue without Pay-to-Win.** Optional rewarded ads, cosmetic purchases, character purchases, and a Remove Ads option. No gameplay advantage sold for money.

## 4. Target audience

- Mobile players who enjoy skill-based arcade/sports games.
- Short-session players (commute, breaks) and longer-session players chasing high scores.
- Players who value fair competition (leaderboards, friends challenges).
- **Not** targeted: players seeking pay-to-win advantages or paywalled progression.

## 5. Unique selling points (USPs)

- Free movement on an open snowy mountain — **no fixed three-lane system**.
- On-the-fly transformation between snow vehicles (double-tap), each with distinct characteristics and balanced usage duration.
- Friends challenges on the **same generated track**, enabling fair, comparable competition.
- Original cartoon-3D visual identity (see Creative Rule below).
- Equal character abilities — identity and cosmetics, not power, differentiate characters.

## 6. Creative rule (mandatory)

**NO GENERIC AI DESIGN.** The game must have an original and recognizable identity.

Avoid:
- Generic AI-generated visual styles.
- Repetitive/generic logos.
- Generic interfaces and templates.
- Unoriginal characters.
- Unnecessary visual effects.
- Copying existing games.

Every visual and UI decision must be reviewed against this rule. See `UI_UX.md`.

## 7. Game feel targets (qualitative, to be validated in prototype)

- Controls feel responsive within the first 10 seconds.
- Drifting feels skill-based, not random.
- Speed increase feels exciting, not punishing.
- Crashes feel fair and readable.
- Recovery / continue flow (rewarded ad) feels like a choice, not a tax.

## 8. Success metrics (to be defined with numbers later)

- Day-1 / Day-7 / Day-30 retention.
- Average session length.
- Rewarded ad opt-in and completion rate.
- Crash-to-continue conversion.
- Leaderboard participation rate.
- Crystal economy balance (earn vs. spend).

Specific numeric targets are **OPEN DECISIONS** — see `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.

## 9. Out of scope for v1 (tentative)

- Multiplayer real-time racing (v1 uses async friends challenges on identical tracks).
- Non-snow environments.
- PC/console ports.
- Any Pay-to-Win mechanic.

These are tentative and subject to revision via documented decision.
